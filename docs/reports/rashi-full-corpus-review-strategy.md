# Rashi translation-quality campaign, Step 5: full-corpus review strategy

Step 5 of the campaign governed by the September-session directive (see
`docs/reports/rashi-translation-quality-plan.md` for Step 1,
`docs/reports/rashi-translation-risk-audit.md` for Step 2,
`docs/reports/rashi-translation-style-guide.md` for Step 3,
`docs/reports/rashi-pilot-step4-final-report.md` for Step 4). This document
formalizes the executable, deterministic full-corpus review strategy Step 4
recommended and the user authorized: **HYBRID REVIEW**. **This document
makes no translation edits, no Hebrew edits, and reviews no entry.** It
defines the batching, tooling, and quality-control lifecycle that Step 6
will execute, and it does not change the truth of the completed Step 4
report - all Step 4 numbers (200 reviewed, 22 changed, 22/22 second-passed,
177/11/9/2/0/1 disposition split) stand exactly as reported.

## Methodology: HYBRID REVIEW

Adopted under the exact operator decisions the governing directive fixed:

1. **Entry-by-entry review is the default** for all 8,654 remaining
   UNREVIEWED entries.
2. **No entry may be marked VERIFIED by risk score, historical bucket,
   string similarity, or absence of a warning.** Automated signals inform
   priority and candidate generation only, never a verdict - the Step 4
   pilot proved this is necessary: risk tier was a weak defect predictor
   (10% high-risk vs. 8% zero-risk changed rate), and 27% of confirmed
   defects (6/22) carried zero automated risk signal at all.
3. **Cluster assistance may only generate candidates for individual
   review.** No cluster output is ever written back to the inventory
   without a full entry-level semantic pass.
4. **No cluster may automatically change or verify an entry.**
5. **The two currently authorized cluster-assisted candidate families**
   (both with direct pilot evidence, see Step 4's report):
   - the fabricated `"New comment:"` scaffold phrase (4/4 pilot instances
     confirmed genuine fabrications; a complete, deterministic literal-
     string search, not a heuristic).
   - cross-entry word anticipation (7 pilot instances, all confirmed
     genuine; candidates generated by reusing the Step 2 `OVEREXPLAINED`
     signal, which had 100% precision, n=3, in the pilot).
6. **Other cluster families require evidence from later direct review**
   before being added - none are authorized by this document.
7. **Every changed or BLOCKED entry requires a complete, independent
   second semantic pass**, exactly as all 22 Step 4 changes and the 1
   BLOCKED entry received.
8. **Every entry must ultimately receive its own supported disposition**
   from the six-value vocabulary (VERIFIED, MINOR_EDIT,
   SUBSTANTIVE_REPAIR, RETRANSLATE, DUPLICATION_OR_CONTAMINATION,
   BLOCKED) - no entry may be left unreviewed at campaign close.

Per explicit instruction, Step 6 will **not** run as ~173 separate
50-entry PRs (the pilot's small experimental batch size, chosen for
close first-pass calibration, not production throughput). Step 5 instead
builds a safer, larger, contiguous-daf batch plan.

## Batching contract

Generator: `modules/yoma/scripts/plan_rashi_full_corpus_batches.py`.
Validator: `modules/yoma/scripts/validate_rashi_full_corpus_batches.py`.
Output: `docs/reports/data/rashi-full-corpus-review-batches.json`.

### Rules (all enforced by the validator, not merely documented)

- Contiguous daf only; a batch is always a single unbroken run of daf in
  corpus order.
- A batch never crosses a perek boundary, even if that means stopping
  short of a size target.
- No entry is ever assigned to more than one batch; no UNREVIEWED entry
  is ever omitted; none of the 200 frozen pilot-cohort entries is ever
  reassigned into a batch.
- Hard ceilings, always binding regardless of tier: **350 entries per
  batch, 8 daf per batch.**
- Density tier (most restrictive tier among a batch's member daf wins)
  sets a tighter cap below the hard ceiling:

  | Tier | Definition | Max entries | Max daf |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `dense` | >=50% of the daf's entries carry a nonzero Step 2 riskScore | 180 | 4 |
  | `normal` | everything not `dense` or `low_defect` | 300 | 6 |
  | `low_defect` | <10% of the daf's entries carry a nonzero riskScore | 350 | 8 |

  The `dense` threshold was not chosen arbitrarily: the corpus's nonzero-
  riskScore fraction is empirically bimodal with a clean gap at 0.3-0.5,
  and the resulting 34-daf `dense` set matches exactly the union of
  Step 1's independently-derived `known-needs-reconstruction` (25 daf) and
  `known-needs-realignment` (9 daf) buckets - a strong cross-check between
  two independently built classification systems.
- Any batch whose **estimated** changed-translation count exceeds 40 is
  recursively halved by daf until it no longer does (or until it is down
  to a single daf, at which point it is flagged `needsSplitReview` for
  human judgment rather than force-split further). The estimate uses
  each entry's historical-provenance bucket's own pilot-observed changed
  rate (`content-reviewed` 11%, `known-needs-realignment` 4%,
  `known-needs-reconstruction` 14%) where that bucket's pilot sample was
  large enough to trust (n>=20); small-sample buckets (`narrow-fix-only`
  n=2, `checked-no-fix-needed` n=9) fall back to the pilot's overall 11%
  rate instead of their own noisy small-sample rate, since a raw
  small-sample rate initially overstated one real batch's expected
  changes by nearly 2x before this correction.

Running the generator against the current corpus produces:

- **41 batches**, covering **8,654 of 8,654** remaining UNREVIEWED
  entries exactly once (0 duplicates, 0 omissions, 0 pilot-entry
  reassignments).
- Batch size: **min 64, max 297, median 244, average 211.1** entries.
- All 173 daf covered exactly once; all 8 perakim represented; no batch

- **0 batches flagged `needsSplitReview`** - the recursive-halving rule

committed file is itself the staleness check

## Per-entry review requirements (every remaining entry, no exceptions)

Every one of the 8,654 remaining entries must eventually be reviewed
against:

- its own Hebrew (`he`),
- its linked Gemara/Mishnah line(s) (`linkedGemaraLineIds`, resolved to
  full Hebrew/English/literal text),
- surrounding Gemara context (a window before and after the linked
  line(s)),
- neighboring Rashi entries on the same daf (relevant for cross-entry
  word-anticipation checks in particular),
- the style guide (`docs/reports/rashi-translation-style-guide.md`),
- the terminology registry
  (`docs/reports/data/rashi-terminology-registry.json`).

Every entry receives exactly one final disposition from the six-value
vocabulary, with concise, specific evidence (citing actual Hebrew
wording or context, never a generic statement). No automated tool may
populate a final disposition - every disposition in Step 6 is written by
a reviewer (human, or an AI acting as a human reviewer, reading and
reasoning the way a human would), exactly as Step 4 required.

## Batch quality-control lifecycle (mandatory for every Step 6 batch PR)

1. **First pass.** Review every entry in the batch; propose a
   disposition; record evidence; propose replacement English only when
   the evidence justifies it.
2. **Second pass.** Independently re-review 100% of entries whose
   first-pass disposition is MINOR_EDIT, SUBSTANTIVE_REPAIR, RETRANSLATE,
   DUPLICATION_OR_CONTAMINATION, or BLOCKED - re-derived from Hebrew and
   context again, not merely re-checked against the first pass's own
   reasoning, exactly as all 22 Step 4 changes and its 1 BLOCKED entry
   were second-passed.
3. **Blind QA.** Independently re-review a deterministic minimum 10%
   sample of provisionally-VERIFIED entries, selected without reference
   to risk score or first-pass reasoning, drawn from both signaled
   (nonzero riskScore) and zero-risk entries.
4. **Escalation rule.** If blind QA finds any entry needing repair,
   retranslation, contamination correction, or a structural stop, the
   batch may not merge until **all** provisionally-VERIFIED entries in
   that batch receive a second pass. Systematic minor-edit drift found
   during blind QA must be documented in the batch report, and the blind
   QA sample must be expanded for that batch. A failed QA result may
   never be hidden by silently replacing the sampled entry with a
   different one.

First-pass and second-pass decisions are recorded as separate fields in
every review record (see contract below), never merged into one
"final" field with no first-pass trace.

### Review-record contract (component 3)

`docs/reports/data/rashi-review-record-contract.json` is a hand-authored
description document (matching the repository's existing convention for
`rashi-terminology-registry.json`), not executable JSON Schema. It
defines the exact shape of one Step 6 review record - `batchId`,
`entryId`, `daf`, `hebrew` (immutable), `originalEnglish` (immutable),
`proposedEnglish`, `firstPassDisposition`, `defectTags`,
`firstPassEvidence`, `secondPass` (`required`, `status`, `evidence`,
`finalEnglish`), `blindQA` (`selected`, `result`, `evidence`),
`finalDisposition`, `structuralStop`, `repairPR`, `finalVerificationSHA`
- plus the `batchReportTotals` fields every batch report must carry and
the nine exact `rejectionRules` `validate_rashi_review_records.py`
implements literally.

### Systemic-candidate generation (component 5)

Reuses existing signals rather than reinventing detectors:

- **`new_comment_scaffold`**: exact literal-string search for
  `"New comment:"` across all UNREVIEWED entries' English. **584
  candidates** in the live corpus (consistent in magnitude with a
  full-corpus grep finding 622 raw occurrences of the string, including
  the 4 already-fixed pilot instances and non-entry-boundary
  occurrences).
- **`cross_entry_word_anticipation`**: reuses the Step 2 `OVEREXPLAINED`
  risk signal as the primary candidate list (chosen over a naive
  length-ratio heuristic, which does not work here - the defect family
  splits into two opposite-direction sub-patterns that already overlap
  differently-precision existing signals), with each candidate's
  immediate daf-neighbors attached for reviewer context. **155
  candidates.**

Neither list carries a disposition, a repair, or any other verdict field
- every candidate still requires the full entry-level semantic review
method before any change is made.

## Review-packet design (Step 6)

`modules/yoma/scripts/generate_rashi_review_packets.py --batch-id
<id> --out <path>` extends Step 4's pilot packet generator
(`generate_rashi_pilot_packets.py`) to accept any batch from the full-
corpus plan instead of only the frozen pilot cohort. For every entry in
the named batch it assembles:

- exact Hebrew (`he`) and current English (`en`),
- linked Gemara Hebrew/English/literal text, plus a two-line context
  window before and after,
- neighboring Rashi entries on the same daf,
- risk signals, risk score, and historical provenance
  (`priorReviewDepth`) from the live inventory (a batch-plan entry
  carries only an id, so all risk detail is pulled from the live
  inventory rather than duplicated into the batch plan itself),
- applicable style-guide sections (computed per entry via the same
  stratum conditions `select_rashi_pilot_cohort.py` uses for its own
  sampling pools - sacrificial/priesthood/purity vocabulary, short-gloss/
  long-explanation length, narrative-connector, multiple-linked-lines,
  terminology-variance, historical-provenance - applied directly to one
  entry rather than accumulated into a corpus-wide sampling pool),
- applicable terminology-registry entries,
- a **blank review record whose immutable fields (`batchId`, `entryId`,
  `daf`, `hebrew`, `originalEnglish`) are pre-filled from the live
  corpus** and whose judgment fields (`proposedEnglish`,
  `firstPassDisposition`, `defectTags`, `firstPassEvidence`,
  `secondPass`, `blindQA`, `finalDisposition`, `structuralStop`,
  `repairPR`, `finalVerificationSHA`) start null/empty - so a filled-in
  review block is directly a valid record against the contract above,
  with no reshaping step in between.

This tool assembles context only; it never reads Hebrew, compares it to
English, or draws a semantic conclusion, exactly like its Step 4
predecessor. `--out` has no default, so it can never silently leave a
generated packet file in `docs/reports/data/` - Step 6 batch PRs generate
their own packet as a working artifact when a batch review begins.

## The one BLOCKED Hebrew-source entry: `rashi-yoma-009b-001`

Full evidence record: `docs/reports/data/rashi-source-blockers.json`.

- **Not edited in Step 5** - Hebrew remains exactly as Step 4 left it.
- **Confirmed upstream, not an ingestion bug**: a fresh, read-only fetch
  of the live `talmud.dev` API during Step 5 evidence-gathering shows the
  identical defect (a `<span class="five">` tag missing its opening `<`)
  present in `talmud.dev`'s own current data at this exact line; every
  other tag in the same API response for the same daf is well-formed and
  was correctly stripped by the existing ingestion script, ruling out a
  systemic ingestion defect.
- **Minimum safe correction identified** (strip the 19-character literal
  artifact `span class="five">`, matching exactly what the existing,
  unmodified ingestion pipeline would have produced had the upstream data
  included the missing `<`) but **not applied** - this is a separate,
  narrowly-scoped source-repair task, out of this campaign's task-type
  registry (`docs-tooling`, `rashi-translation-review`) and out of Step
  5's scope. `modules/yoma/assets/talmuddev/9b.json` remains untouched.
- **Planning status**: marked `SOURCE_REPAIR_REQUIRED` in the planning
  layer (`rashi-source-blockers.json`) while retaining its valid Step 4
  campaign disposition of `BLOCKED` in the inventory - no change to the
  inventory happened in Step 5.
- Not present in any of the 41 Step 6 batches (it is already `REVIEWED`,
  and batches are built exclusively from UNREVIEWED entries). The
  blocker record documents a recommendation - not an action taken - to
  reset this one entry to UNREVIEWED and fold it into a small follow-up
  review once the separate source-repair PR lands.
